Gentle, honest guides to what the Gita teaches about the feelings we all carry — with the original Sanskrit, translations and simple meanings.

Verses

  • 2.3 — You are stronger than this moment is telling you. When you're ready, you can rise.
  • 2.7 — Even Arjuna — a great warrior — admitted he was confused and asked for help. Not knowing is where wisdom begins.
  • 2.11 — Krishna's first teaching begins with grief — not to dismiss it, but to open a wider view of it.
  • 2.13 — You have already changed many times and remained yourself. Change is not the end of you.
  • 2.14 — What you feel right now is real — and it is also passing. Like weather, it will change.
  • 2.15 — There is a part of you that can stay steady even while life moves around you.
  • 2.20 — Something in you is untouched by what happens to you — deeper than any loss or label.
  • 2.22 — Endings can also be a kind of renewal — the old giving way so something new can begin.
  • 2.38 — Do what is in front of you without letting the scoreboard decide how you feel about yourself.
  • 2.40 — No sincere effort is wasted. Even a small step counts.
  • 2.47 — Put your whole heart into what you do, then loosen your grip on how it turns out. The outcome was never fully yours to hold.
  • 2.48 — Steadiness isn't about winning or losing. It's about staying yourself in both.
  • 2.50 — Yoga isn't only on a mat. It's doing ordinary things with care, attention and a light heart.
  • 2.56 — Steadiness is possible — not by never feeling, but by not being ruled by every feeling.
  • 2.62 — What we keep replaying in our minds slowly grows into wanting — and unmet wanting often turns into anger.
  • 2.63 — Anger clouds what we know. In its grip we forget who we are and what we value.
  • 2.66 — Happiness grows from peace, and peace grows from a settled mind. Start with the settling.
  • 2.70 — Be like the ocean: let wants and worries flow in without letting them overflow you.
  • 2.71 — Peace often arrives when we hold things — and ourselves — a little more lightly.
  • 3.8 — When everything feels stuck, the next small action is often the way through.
  • 3.19 — Keep doing what matters, without needing every effort to be noticed or rewarded.
  • 3.35 — Your own path, walked imperfectly, is worth more than someone else's path walked flawlessly.
  • 3.37 — The restlessness inside — wanting and anger — is often the real obstacle, more than anything outside us.
  • 4.36 — No mistake puts you beyond the reach of understanding and a fresh start.
  • 4.38 — Clarity comes in its own time — and it comes from within.
  • 4.39 — Trust, steady effort and a little self-restraint open the way to understanding — and understanding to peace.
  • 5.10 — You can live fully in the world without letting it soak into you — like a lotus leaf in water.
  • 5.22 — Quick pleasures fade quickly. Lasting contentment is found somewhere quieter.
  • 5.29 — You are not alone. In the Gita's vision, the Divine is the well-wishing friend of every being — including you.
  • 6.5 — You can be your own friend. The way you speak to yourself can lift you, or pull you down.
  • 6.6 — The same mind that worries you can become your greatest support, with patience and care.
  • 6.7 — Praise and criticism both pass through. Your calm doesn't have to depend on either.
  • 6.17 — Rest is part of the path. Balance in food, work and sleep is itself a spiritual practice.
  • 6.19 — A settled mind is like a flame where no wind blows — still, warm, and clear.
  • 6.26 — The mind will wander. That's fine. The practice is simply bringing it back — again and again.
  • 6.32 — Compassion means feeling another's joy and pain as if it were your own.
  • 6.34 — Even Arjuna found his mind as hard to hold as the wind. If yours feels that way, you are in good company.
  • 6.35 — Yes, the mind is hard to steady — and yet, with gentle practice and letting go, it slowly becomes calmer.
  • 6.40 — Sincere effort is never lost — even when you fall short. Krishna calls Arjuna 'tāta', a tender word, like 'my dear'.
  • 7.8 — The sacred is not far away. It's in the taste of water, the light of the morning, the strength in you.
  • 9.22 — You don't have to carry everything alone. In the Gita's vision, those who turn toward the Divine are cared for.
  • 9.26 — What matters is the love behind an offering, not its size. A small, sincere gesture is enough.
  • 10.20 — In the Gita's vision, the sacred lives in your own heart. You carry something precious within you.
  • 12.13 — Friendliness, compassion and forgiveness — these are the marks of a beautiful heart.
  • 12.15 — Peace is mutual: not disturbing others, and not being easily disturbed by them.
  • 12.18 — Inner balance means not letting people's treatment of you decide your peace.
  • 15.7 — You are not an accident or an afterthought. In the Gita's view, you are part of something eternal.
  • 16.1 — The first quality the Gita lists in a noble nature is fearlessness. Courage is the ground other virtues grow from.
  • 17.15 — Before you speak, ask: is it true, is it kind, is it helpful?
  • 18.48 — Nothing worth doing is flawless. Every fire has some smoke — keep going anyway.
  • 18.63 — After all his teaching, Krishna doesn't command — he asks Arjuna to reflect deeply and then choose freely.
  • 18.66 — ‘Mā śucaḥ’ — do not grieve. When everything feels too much, there is a place to rest.
  • 18.78 — Wisdom and willing action together — that's where good things grow.
