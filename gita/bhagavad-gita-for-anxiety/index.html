The Bhagavad Gita for an anxious mind

The Bhagavad Gita opens with a man in distress. Arjuna's hands tremble, his mouth goes dry and he cannot think clearly about what to do. Many readers recognise themselves in that moment.

The Gita does not describe anxiety in clinical terms, and it is not a treatment for it. What it offers is perspective: a way of relating to a restless mind, to uncertainty about the future, and to the pressure of outcomes we cannot fully control.

A gentle note: the Bhagavad Gita is a spiritual and philosophical text, not a medical treatment. If anxiety is affecting your daily life, please consider speaking with a doctor or mental-health professional. In India, Tele-MANAS offers free support 24×7 at 14416.

How the Gita approaches this

Again and again, the Gita returns to two ideas. First, the mind is genuinely hard to steady — even Arjuna says it is like trying to hold the wind. Second, steadiness grows slowly through practice (abhyāsa) and letting go (vairāgya), and through placing our attention on our actions rather than on outcomes.

Verses to reflect on

Bhagavad Gita · Chapter 6, Verse 34

चञ्चलं हि मनः कृष्ण प्रमाथि बलवद्दृढम्।
तस्याहं निग्रहं मन्ये वायोरिव सुदुष्करम्॥

cañcalaṁ hi manaḥ kṛṣṇa pramāthi balavad dṛḍham
tasyāhaṁ nigrahaṁ manye vāyor iva su-duṣkaram

Translation

The mind is restless, Krishna — turbulent, powerful and stubborn. I think it is as hard to control as the wind.

हिन्दी भावार्थ

हे कृष्ण, यह मन बड़ा चंचल, विचलित करने वाला, बलवान और हठी है। इसे वश में करना मुझे वायु को रोकने जैसा कठिन लगता है।

In simple words Interpretation

Even Arjuna found his mind as hard to hold as the wind. If yours feels that way, you are in good company.

When this may help: When you feel you're 'bad' at calming down, or your thoughts feel impossible to manage.

A question to sit with: What would change if you stopped blaming yourself for a restless mind?

Bhagavad Gita · Chapter 6, Verse 35

असंशयं महाबाहो मनो दुर्निग्रहं चलम्।
अभ्यासेन तु कौन्तेय वैराग्येण च गृह्यते॥

asaṁśayaṁ mahā-bāho mano durnigrahaṁ calam
abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate

Translation

Without doubt, mighty-armed one, the mind is restless and hard to restrain. But through practice and non-attachment, it can be steadied.

हिन्दी भावार्थ

हे महाबाहो, निःसंदेह मन चंचल है और कठिनता से वश में होता है। परंतु हे कौन्तेय, अभ्यास और वैराग्य से इसे वश में किया जा सकता है।

In simple words Interpretation

Yes, the mind is hard to steady — and yet, with gentle practice and letting go, it slowly becomes calmer.

When this may help: When overthinking feels endless and you need both validation and a way forward.

A question to sit with: What is one small practice you could repeat daily to steady your mind?

Bhagavad Gita · Chapter 2, Verse 47

कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन।
मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि॥

karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana
mā karma-phala-hetur bhūr mā te saṅgo 'stv akarmaṇi

Translation

You have a right to your actions, but never to their fruits. Do not let the results be your motive, and do not be attached to inaction either.

हिन्दी भावार्थ

तुम्हारा अधिकार केवल कर्म करने में है, उसके फल में कभी नहीं। इसलिए फल को कर्म का कारण मत बनाओ, और कर्म न करने में भी आसक्ति मत रखो।

In simple words Interpretation

Put your whole heart into what you do, then loosen your grip on how it turns out. The outcome was never fully yours to hold.

When this may help: When you are anxious about results, exhausted from trying to control everything, or afraid to begin because it might not work out.

A question to sit with: What are you trying to control that may not be yours to control?

Bhagavad Gita · Chapter 6, Verse 26

यतो यतो निश्चरति मनश्चञ्चलमस्थिरम्।
ततस्ततो नियम्यैतदात्मन्येव वशं नयेत्॥

yato yato niścarati manaś cañcalam asthiram
tatas tato niyamyaitad ātmany eva vaśaṁ nayet

Translation

Wherever the restless, unsteady mind wanders, from there one should gently draw it back and bring it home to the self.

हिन्दी भावार्थ

यह चंचल और अस्थिर मन जहाँ-जहाँ भटकता है, वहाँ-वहाँ से उसे रोककर बार-बार आत्मा में ही स्थिर करना चाहिए।

In simple words Interpretation

The mind will wander. That's fine. The practice is simply bringing it back — again and again.

When this may help: When you're frustrated that you can't stop overthinking.

A question to sit with: Where does your mind go most often when it wanders?

Bhagavad Gita · Chapter 2, Verse 14

मात्रास्पर्शास्तु कौन्तेय शीतोष्णसुखदुःखदाः।
आगमापायिनोऽनित्यास्तांस्तितिक्षस्व भारत॥

mātrā-sparśās tu kaunteya śītoṣṇa-sukha-duḥkha-dāḥ
āgamāpāyino 'nityās tāṁs titikṣasva bhārata

Translation

Contact with the world brings cold and heat, pleasure and pain. These come and go; they are not permanent. Learn to bear them patiently, Arjuna.

हिन्दी भावार्थ

हे कौन्तेय, इंद्रियों और विषयों के संपर्क से सर्दी-गर्मी और सुख-दुख आते हैं। ये आते-जाते रहते हैं, स्थायी नहीं हैं। हे भारत, इन्हें धैर्य से सहन करो।

In simple words Interpretation

What you feel right now is real — and it is also passing. Like weather, it will change.

When this may help: When a feeling seems like it will last forever, or a hard season feels endless.

A question to sit with: What is one feeling you've carried before that eventually passed?

A simple practice

For two minutes, count your breaths up to ten. Each time your mind wanders, notice where it went and gently start again at one. The returning is the practice.
