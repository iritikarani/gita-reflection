The Bhagavad Gita on overthinking

Overthinking often feels like trying to solve tomorrow's problems tonight. The Gita has a surprisingly practical view of this: the mind wanders by nature, and the work is not to force it still but to keep returning, patiently.

Chapter 6 in particular reads almost like a meditation guide, written for someone who has tried to calm their thoughts and found it hard.

How the Gita approaches this

The Gita traces how dwelling on something turns into attachment, then craving, then frustration (2.62–63). It doesn't shame the overthinker — Krishna agrees with Arjuna that the mind is restless — but offers the gentle discipline of returning attention to the present, again and again.

Verses to reflect on

Bhagavad Gita · Chapter 6, Verse 26

यतो यतो निश्चरति मनश्चञ्चलमस्थिरम्।
ततस्ततो नियम्यैतदात्मन्येव वशं नयेत्॥

yato yato niścarati manaś cañcalam asthiram
tatas tato niyamyaitad ātmany eva vaśaṁ nayet

Translation

Wherever the restless, unsteady mind wanders, from there one should gently draw it back and bring it home to the self.

हिन्दी भावार्थ

यह चंचल और अस्थिर मन जहाँ-जहाँ भटकता है, वहाँ-वहाँ से उसे रोककर बार-बार आत्मा में ही स्थिर करना चाहिए।

In simple words Interpretation

The mind will wander. That's fine. The practice is simply bringing it back — again and again.

When this may help: When you're frustrated that you can't stop overthinking.

A question to sit with: Where does your mind go most often when it wanders?

Bhagavad Gita · Chapter 6, Verse 35

असंशयं महाबाहो मनो दुर्निग्रहं चलम्।
अभ्यासेन तु कौन्तेय वैराग्येण च गृह्यते॥

asaṁśayaṁ mahā-bāho mano durnigrahaṁ calam
abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate

Translation

Without doubt, mighty-armed one, the mind is restless and hard to restrain. But through practice and non-attachment, it can be steadied.

हिन्दी भावार्थ

हे महाबाहो, निःसंदेह मन चंचल है और कठिनता से वश में होता है। परंतु हे कौन्तेय, अभ्यास और वैराग्य से इसे वश में किया जा सकता है।

In simple words Interpretation

Yes, the mind is hard to steady — and yet, with gentle practice and letting go, it slowly becomes calmer.

When this may help: When overthinking feels endless and you need both validation and a way forward.

A question to sit with: What is one small practice you could repeat daily to steady your mind?

Bhagavad Gita · Chapter 2, Verse 62

ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते।
सङ्गात्सञ्जायते कामः कामात्क्रोधोऽभिजायते॥

dhyāyato viṣayān puṁsaḥ saṅgas teṣūpajāyate
saṅgāt sañjāyate kāmaḥ kāmāt krodho 'bhijāyate

Translation

When a person dwells on objects of the senses, attachment to them arises. From attachment comes desire, and from desire comes anger.

हिन्दी भावार्थ

विषयों का चिंतन करने से उनमें आसक्ति पैदा होती है। आसक्ति से कामना जन्म लेती है, और कामना से क्रोध उत्पन्न होता है।

In simple words Interpretation

What we keep replaying in our minds slowly grows into wanting — and unmet wanting often turns into anger.

When this may help: When you notice yourself replaying something again and again, or when anger seems to come from nowhere.

A question to sit with: What thought have you been returning to again and again?

Bhagavad Gita · Chapter 6, Verse 19

यथा दीपो निवातस्थो नेङ्गते सोपमा स्मृता।
योगिनो यतचित्तस्य युञ्जतो योगमात्मनः॥

yathā dīpo nivāta-stho neṅgate sopamā smṛtā
yogino yata-cittasya yuñjato yogam ātmanaḥ

Translation

As a lamp in a windless place does not flicker — that is the image used for the disciplined mind of one practising meditation.

हिन्दी भावार्थ

जैसे वायुरहित स्थान में रखा दीपक नहीं डगमगाता, वैसी ही उपमा ध्यान में लगे हुए संयमित चित्त वाले योगी की दी गई है।

In simple words Interpretation

A settled mind is like a flame where no wind blows — still, warm, and clear.

When this may help: When your mind feels scattered and you long for a moment of stillness.

A question to sit with: What is the 'wind' that keeps making your mind flicker?

Bhagavad Gita · Chapter 4, Verse 38

न हि ज्ञानेन सदृशं पवित्रमिह विद्यते।
तत्स्वयं योगसंसिद्धः कालेनात्मनि विन्दति॥

na hi jñānena sadṛśaṁ pavitram iha vidyate
tat svayaṁ yoga-saṁsiddhaḥ kālenātmani vindati

Translation

Nothing in this world purifies like knowledge. One who is mature in yoga finds it within oneself, in time.

हिन्दी भावार्थ

इस संसार में ज्ञान के समान पवित्र करने वाला कुछ भी नहीं है। योग में सिद्ध व्यक्ति समय आने पर उसे स्वयं अपने भीतर पा लेता है।

In simple words Interpretation

Clarity comes in its own time — and it comes from within.

When this may help: When you're impatient for answers or frustrated that you haven't 'figured it out' yet.

A question to sit with: What are you trying to understand faster than it can be understood?

A simple practice

When you notice yourself replaying a thought, silently name it — "replaying" — and bring your attention to one physical sensation, like your feet on the floor.
